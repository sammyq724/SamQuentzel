Core Story Set

Core Stories

Four behavioral interview questions, each answered in STAR format.

Tell me about a time you took initiative

Situation. Over the summer, I was building really impactful software, yet almost no one knew about it, not even my close friends. I realized that if nobody knows about my product, I haven't really built anything. Building was easy for me. Telling people about it was much harder.

Task. I needed to push past my self-doubt and present my work publicly, even though I kept comparing myself to people building far bigger things.

Action. When I saw IdeaBounce, a two-minute pitch competition for a $200 prize in front of a live crowd, hosted by WashU's Skandalaris Center, I signed up immediately, before I could talk myself out of it. Then I put in the work to prepare my pitch.

Result. I was nervous, but I won. It was the jump start my sophomore fall needed, and it taught me that growth only happens outside your comfort zone.

Tell me about a time you worked with data

Situation. This past summer, while working at my father's wholesale plumbing supply company, I realized how much data I had access to: decades of customer history, item sales, and more.

Task. I wanted to use it to build a prediction model that takes a customer's request for material (“Can I please get a price for 5 - 3 inch brass elbows?”) and returns the exact item ID from our catalog.

Action. I compiled tens of thousands of customer requests line by line and matched each one to the correct item. I then trained the model using statistical methods combined with plumbing rules I gathered from online research and feedback from our sales team.

Result. The model reached 90% accuracy across 10,000 trials. It taught me to take advantage of the resources and connections in front of me, because everyone has unique advantages worth using.

Tell me about a time you persuaded someone to see your point of view

Situation. This past summer, I wanted to bring AI software I had built into my father's plumbing supply company, which he has owned for 40 years. He and the experienced inside sales team were against it. They didn't understand AI, and saying no was easier than trying to.

Task. I had to convince baby boomers in an old-school distribution industry that AI was worth adopting. I had to persuade an old dog to try a new trick.

Action. I explained the software in plain vocabulary they understood and walked them through how it worked. Most importantly, I made sure the product delivered real results they could see for themselves.

Result. My father went from doubting AI to wanting to go all in on it. I learned two things: speak your audience's language and back it up with results, and let people new to AI treat it as a black box.

Tell me about a time you gave a presentation

Situation. As a freshman, I took Management 1000, a course required for all business students that ends in a large case competition. The top four teams presented in the finals to company executives, the dean, the assistant dean, and the entire freshman business class.

Task. My team needed to deliver a strong preliminary presentation to earn a spot in the finals, and then perform in front of roughly 500 people.

Action. We put in long overtime hours preparing, and I worked hard to master my lines despite my nerves.

Result. We advanced to the finals, and I delivered. That experience has changed how I approach every presentation since. Now, whenever I feel nervous, I remind myself that I've already presented to 500 people, so this is nothing. Doing hard things only makes you stronger.
