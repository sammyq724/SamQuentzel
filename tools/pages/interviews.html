Interview with my mother

A conversation with my mother, Sandy Quentzel, about her path from accounting to serving as mayor of Roslyn Harbor.

What I learned

My mother's path into accounting began by chance at a summer job, guided by a mentor she still remembers by name. Years later, she became mayor of Roslyn Harbor by answering an email for an unpaid volunteer role. Hearing that made me realize I don't need my whole future figured out right now. What matters more is building real skills and relationships and being open when unexpected opportunities appear.

Her COVID story also made me rethink what leadership means. It isn't about a title. It's about stepping up when people depend on you. I've watched her lead my whole life, but I never fully saw how her experiences shaped her. As I pursue a future in entrepreneurship and business leadership, I want to follow her advice: build genuine connections, treat every interaction like it matters, and work hard enough that I'm ready when my own unexpected door opens.

Interview with my father

A conversation with my father, Andy Quentzel, about hard work and what 40 years of owning a plumbing supply company in Brooklyn taught him.

What I learned

Interviewing my dad, Andy Quentzel, made me realize how much I've learned from him about what hard work really means. I used to think working hard meant putting in eight hours a day and checking things off a list. But growing up with someone who wakes up at 3 in the morning and gets home around sunset showed me a completely different standard. Watching that every day growing up shaped my work ethic more than any lesson in school could.

His biggest lesson has always been that there's no free lunch. Nothing is given to you; everything is earned. He believes you have to create your own destiny and that it's up to you to surround yourself with good people. Hearing him put those beliefs into words, especially his advice to be a sponge (learn from people smarter than you), reminded me that he has been guiding me in the right direction my entire life.
