Things I’ve built and pitched

Three projects, in the order I would talk about them: an AI system running inside a real business, a case competition final, and an iOS game.

July 2026 to present Brooklyn, NY

AI Prediction Model for Plumbing Supplies

A contractor emails “Can I please get a price for 5 - 3 inch brass elbows?” Someone on the inside sales team has to know which of more than 20,000 catalog items that means. I built a system that does it automatically for my father’s wholesale plumbing supply company.

90%accuracy across more than 10,000 test predictions
115+purchase emails routed through the pipeline every day
~20 hrssaved for the sales team each week
  • Engineered an AI-driven matching system that interprets quote and purchase order requests, regardless of slang, phrasing, or formatting, and maps each line item to the correct SKU in the company’s ERP system.
  • Built the automated intake pipeline with Make.com and Render.
  • Architected the full AI stack: Claude Sonnet 5 for document and image parsing and core reasoning, DeepSeek V4-Pro for relevance filtering, and Fable 5.1 with Opus 5 for model development and software engineering.
  • Pitched the product at IdeaBounce, a two-minute pitch competition hosted by WashU’s Skandalaris Center, and won.

Freshman year Management 1000 · Team of five

LyftLink: Case Competition Finalist

Management 1000 ends in a case competition for the whole freshman business class. Our case was Lyft, which at the time earned nearly all of its revenue in the United States while Uber was growing abroad. Our answer was LyftLink, an entry into Africa through Nairobi that organizes the city’s matatu minibus network instead of replacing it, using digital payments, route optimization, and advertising.

We were one of four teams to advance to the finals, where I presented to roughly 500 people, including company executives and the dean.
